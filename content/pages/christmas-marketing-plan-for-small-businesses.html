Christmas Is Closer Than You Think: Your 6-Week Festive Marketing Plan

Published by MM Digital

November 2026

It might only be the start of November, but your customers are already thinking about Christmas. They're saving ideas, comparing prices, and quietly deciding which businesses they'll spend with over the next few weeks. By the time December arrives, many of those decisions will already be made.

That's why the businesses that do best at Christmas aren't the ones that shout loudest in the final fortnight. They're the ones that plan early, show up consistently, and make it easy for people to buy. If you start now, you have just enough time to do it properly.

Why November is when Christmas is won

Festive shopping doesn't happen in one go. People browse and research for weeks, then buy in bursts: around Black Friday, on payday, and in the last-minute rush before delivery cut-offs. Every one of those moments rewards the businesses people already know and trust.

Advertising also gets more expensive as Christmas gets closer, because everyone is competing for the same attention. Building your audience in November, while costs are lower, means you're not paying peak prices to reach people for the first time in December.

Your week-by-week plan

Week 1 (2 to 8 November): Plan and prepare

Decide what you want to push this Christmas, whether that's best sellers, gift bundles, vouchers or festive bookings, and what offers (if any) you'll run. Note your key dates, check your stock and delivery times, and plan your content so you're not making it up as you go.

Week 2 (9 to 15 November): Warm up your audience

Start talking about Christmas without selling hard. Share behind-the-scenes content, new products, gift ideas and customer favourites. Encourage people to join your mailing list or follow you so they're the first to hear about your offers.

Week 3 (16 to 22 November): Gift guides and early offers

Make choosing easy. Gift guides ("for her", "under £25", "for the person who has everything") work brilliantly on social media, email and your website. An early-bird offer for your loyal customers rewards them and gets sales moving before the Black Friday noise begins.

Week 4 (23 to 29 November): Black Friday week

Black Friday falls on 27 November this year. You don't have to discount heavily to take part. Added value, such as free gift wrapping, a bonus item or free delivery, can work just as well and protects your margins. Whatever you do, tell people in advance so they're ready to buy.

Week 5 (30 November to 6 December): Cyber Monday and Small Business Saturday

Cyber Monday (30 November) is a second chance to reach anyone who missed out, and Small Business Saturday on 5 December is perfect for independent and local businesses. Lean into what makes you different: real people, real service, and money that stays in the community.

Week 6 and beyond (7 December onwards): Deadlines and last-minute shoppers

Make your last order and delivery dates impossible to miss across your website, emails and social media. Once those dates pass, switch to gift vouchers, e-gift cards, click and collect or bookings for the new year, so you keep selling right up to Christmas Eve.

Getting each channel ready

Paid ads

Start with smaller budgets in November to build and test your audiences, then increase spend on the campaigns that are working as the big dates arrive. Retargeting people who've already visited your website is one of the most cost-effective things you can do at Christmas. If you've read our post on why most businesses waste their ad spend, the same rules apply, just with higher stakes.

Social media

A festive feed shouldn't just be a list of sales. Mix gift ideas and offers with behind-the-scenes moments, customer stories and a bit of personality. People buy from businesses they feel connected to, and Christmas is the perfect time to show who you are.

Your website

All of that attention leads somewhere, so make sure your website is ready for it. Check it loads quickly on mobile, that your festive offers and delivery dates are clear, and that buying or booking takes as few steps as possible. Our guide to what makes a high converting website is a good checklist.

Email

Your mailing list is made up of people who already like you, which makes it one of your most valuable channels at Christmas. Use it for early access, gift guides and deadline reminders, and keep each email focused on one clear action.

Google Business Profile

If you have a shop, salon, venue or any local presence, update your festive opening hours and add Christmas posts and photos. It's a quick win that helps local customers find you, and we covered it in more detail in our local SEO guide.

The dates to put in your diary

  • Friday 27 November: Black Friday
  • Monday 30 November: Cyber Monday
  • Saturday 5 December: Small Business Saturday
  • Mid December: last posting dates for Christmas delivery (check your courier's dates and share them early)
  • Thursday 24 December: Christmas Eve and the last chance for gift vouchers and e-gift cards

A word of honesty: it's not too late, but it soon will be

Christmas marketing works best when it's planned, not rushed. You don't need to do everything on this list, but the earlier you start, the more each pound and each post will work for you. Leave it until December and you'll be competing for attention at the most expensive point of the year.
